# INC-1902: redis-feeds out of memory

2025-04-29, 08:14-08:32 UTC, sev3, IC: gf, author: gf

## Summary

`redis-feeds` ran out of memory while holding the `transcode` queue.

The trigger was boring, which is usually how these things go. A consumer got stuck, stopped draining the queue, and the backlog kept growing. Redis was configured with `noeviction`, so once `maxmemory` was reached, writes were refused instead of being discarded. That would have been a bad day on its own. Then the pod was OOMKilled, which made it an exciting bad day.

Sentinel promoted a replica in 7 minutes. Service recovered without losing jobs. The queue was ugly, but intact. The system did what it was told, which was the problem.

## Impact

- Transcode queue writes were rejected for 7 minutes.
- `transcoder` jobs were delayed for 11 minutes at peak.
- 184 upload events accumulated behind the blocked `transcode` queue.
- 0 jobs were lost.
- 1 `redis-feeds` pod was OOMKilled.
- 1 alert fired: `KubePodCrashLooping`.

This was a sev3 because the blast radius stayed inside queueing and recovery was automatic, but it still caused visible latency in transcode processing.

## Root cause

The immediate cause was a stuck consumer in the `transcode` queue path. It stopped making progress, so Redis kept accepting queue growth until memory pressure hit the configured ceiling.

The important part is the configuration choice. `redis-feeds` was using `noeviction`, which is a valid setting if the goal is to fail loudly instead of quietly dropping data. We did fail loudly. Then we kept failing until the pod died.

That setting is not wrong. Leaving it in place without a hard guardrail for consumer stalls is wrong. If the queue can stop draining and no one notices until Redis hits `maxmemory`, we have built a trap, not a queue.

Sentinel behaved as expected once the pod died. The failover took 7 minutes, which was slow enough to be annoying and fast enough to avoid data loss. That is not a compliment, just a fact.

## What went well

- Alerting fired on the actual symptom, not on a vague sense of doom.
- Sentinel promotion completed automatically.
- No jobs were lost.
- The queue was recoverable because the backlog stayed in Redis and was not dropped.
- `transcoder` recovery was straightforward once the stuck consumer was restarted.

## What went badly

- We relied on a single consumer path draining a hot queue without a hard stall detector.
- `noeviction` did exactly what it says on the tin, then everyone acted surprised.
- The pod OOMKilled before operator intervention could help.
- Failover took 7 minutes, which is long enough to age a person.
- We did not have a clean operational signal for “queue growth with no drain progress” before Redis memory pressure became the headline.

Strong opinion: do not let a queue grow forever and then act impressed when it eventually fills the box. That is not resilience. That is storage with suspense.
