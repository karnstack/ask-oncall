# INC-1932: 401s for renewed private feed subscribers

2025-06-17, 08:10 UTC to 10:12 UTC, sev2, IC: ay, author: ay

## Summary

On 2025-06-17, renewed private feed subscribers intermittently received 401 responses when fetching their podcast feeds. The issue lasted for about 2 hours and affected the `private-feeds` service in `prod-eu-west`.

The immediate cause was a stalled subscription sync job. A malformed renewal record caused the sync loop to stop advancing, so the allow-list cache did not pick up newly renewed tokens. Subscribers whose renewals were processed in the payment system but not reflected in the cache were treated as unauthorized.

This incident was handled as a privacy-sensitive event. We only confirmed access failures at the feed layer, and we did not find evidence of token exposure. Still, any authentication regression on private feeds must be treated carefully because feed URLs carry subscriber-specific access.

## Impact

- Duration: 2 hours, 2 minutes
- Severity: sev2
- Affected service: `private-feeds`
- User impact: 1,284 renewed subscribers received 401 responses when opening their private RSS feeds
- Retry impact: 3,911 total unauthorized feed requests were returned during the window, mostly from podcast apps retrying automatically
- Scope: impacted subscribers were limited to renewals that should have been admitted by the allow-list cache but had not yet been synced
- Data impact: none observed
- Privacy impact: no evidence of token leakage, no evidence of unauthorized access to private content

## Root cause

The `private-feeds` subscription sync job processes renewal records and updates the allow-list cache used by the feed endpoint. A single malformed renewal record triggered an unhandled parsing failure in the sync loop. The worker did not skip the bad record and continue, it stalled on that input and stopped processing later renewals.

That failure mode exposed two issues:

1. The sync job did not have robust record-level quarantine for malformed renewal data.
2. The allow-list cache had no staleness alert, so we did not notice that the cache stopped advancing until users started reporting 401s.

The job failure itself was not enough to create customer impact. The impact came from the combination of stalled sync processing and missing freshness monitoring.

## What went well

- The 401 increase was visible quickly in the standard service dashboards.
- The on-call response focused on the feed auth path first, which kept us from making unsafe changes to unrelated systems.
- We were able to validate the problem without inspecting or exposing subscriber tokens, which matters for privacy and security.
- Manual restart of the sync worker was effective once the malformed record was isolated.

Do:
- Treat private feed auth failures as privacy-sensitive until proven otherwise.
- Validate the cache state before changing endpoint logic.
- Preserve evidence from logs and dashboards for later review.

Do not:
- Do not assume renewed subscribers are fully synced just because the payment side shows success.
- Do not widen access as a quick fix.
- Do not add ad hoc overrides without a clear expiry and audit trail.

## What went badly

- The sync job had a brittle failure mode, one bad renewal record blocked progress for all later records.
- There was no alert on allow-list cache staleness, so the system failed quietly.
- We did not have a ready-made runbook for selectively quarantining malformed renewal data.
- The first corrective action required manual intervention instead of an automated recovery path.

Do:
- Add alerting on freshness, not just error counts.
- Make ingestion jobs continue past malformed records when safe.
- Keep the remediation path simple and documented.

Do not:
- Do not rely on upstream correctness for every renewal record.
- Do not trust a cache that has not advanced in hours.
- Do not leave private-feed authorization without a freshness signal.
