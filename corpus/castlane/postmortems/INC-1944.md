# INC-1944: Duplicate payouts for 14 creators

2025-07-08, 06:00 to 11:20 UTC, sev2, IC: nd, author: jh

## Summary

At 06:00 UTC on 2025-07-08, the monthly payout batch for June finished starting in `payouts-worker`. Several payout jobs hit provider timeouts while submitting transfers through the payment provider. An operator retried the failed jobs manually from `payouts`, but the retry path did not reuse the original idempotency key. As a result, 14 creators were paid twice.

We caught the issue during reconciliation, after the second wave of transfers had already completed for most affected creators. We reversed or recovered funds for 11 creators the same day. For 3 creators, the funds could not be recovered and were written off by Finance. The underlying fix was to make payout idempotency keyed per creator and payout month, and to require that all retry paths reuse that key.

This was a sev2 because the blast radius was limited to one batch, but it involved direct money movement and required manual recovery work.

## Impact

- 14 creators received duplicate payouts.
- 14 duplicate transfers were created, one extra transfer per affected creator.
- 11 duplicates were recovered successfully.
- 3 duplicates, totaling 4,860.00 USD, were written off.
- Total duplicate exposure was 18,420.00 USD.
- 1 monthly payout batch was delayed by 2 hours and 34 minutes while we reconciled and paused further retries.
- 2 engineers and 1 Finance contact spent most of the business day on review, reversals, and reconciliation.

No creator was left unpaid. No unrelated payout batches were affected.

## Root cause

The batch job created a payout request with an idempotency key derived from the immediate job execution context, not from the creator and payout month. That worked for the normal one-shot flow, but it failed under manual retry.

When the provider timed out, the original request had already reached the payment provider for some creators, but our worker did not receive a success response. The retry was treated as a new request, because the retry code path generated a different key. The payment provider therefore accepted both requests and processed both transfers.

Two contributing factors made this worse:

1. The retry was done manually under time pressure, without a second-person review of the payout request metadata.
2. Our preflight checks verified the amount and destination, but did not verify that the idempotency key matched the original payout attempt for that creator and month.

The key lesson is that payout retries must be safe by default. For money movement, a retry cannot depend on operator memory or on the path that triggered it. The idempotency identity has to be stable and auditable.

## What went well

- Reconciliation found the issue quickly, before the end of the batch window.
- The team paused further retries as soon as duplicate behavior was confirmed.
- We had enough metadata to match the duplicate transfers back to creators and payout month.
- 11 recoveries completed the same day, which reduced the write-off.
- The two-person review expectation was followed during the recovery and verification steps, even though it was not enforced by the software path that caused the issue.

## What went badly

- The retry workflow in `payouts` was not idempotent across provider timeouts.
- Manual retry was too easy to perform without a required confirmation of the original payout identity.
- The system did not surface a strong enough warning that a retried submission could create a second transfer.
- The payout batch had no automatic guard that compared retry keys against the original creator-month payout identity.
- We relied on process, not code, for a control that should have been enforced in the service.
