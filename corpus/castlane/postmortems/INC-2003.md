# INC-2003: Expired certificate on the audio host
2025-10-14, 08:41 to 09:23 UTC, sev2, IC: yu, author: yu

## Summary

At 08:41 UTC, the certificate used by the audio host on `cdn-edge` expired. Playback requests from most podcast apps started failing with TLS errors because clients could not complete the handshake to fetch episode audio. The outage lasted 18 minutes for the expired certificate window, but user-visible playback errors continued until the new certificate was issued and propagated.

The immediate trigger was a cert-manager renewal failure. Renewal attempts were blocked by issuer rate limits after a misconfigured staging loop generated a burst of certificate requests. That loop was only meant to exercise staging, but it was able to target the same issuer used by production. Once the issuer started throttling, cert-manager could not renew the production audio host certificate before expiry.

Alerting came from `KubeClientCertificateExpiration`, but it was not enough on its own because the certificate had already expired by the time the alert was acknowledged. We also saw a rise in edge request failures in Grafana, which matched the user reports coming in through support and social channels.

## Impact

- Audio playback failed for most apps serving Castlane shows through `cdn-edge`.
- 63 percent of audio fetches returned TLS handshake errors during the incident window.
- 1,284,091 playback attempts failed before recovery.
- 4,912 active listeners saw repeated retry loops in the client.
- 318 creator shows had at least one episode unavailable for playback during the outage.
- No data loss occurred, and no uploads, feeds, or payouts were affected.

## Root cause

The root cause was a production certificate renewal failure on the audio host. cert-manager was configured correctly for normal renewals, but it could not complete the renewal because the issuer was rate limiting requests. That rate limit was triggered by a misconfigured staging loop that repeatedly requested certificates against the same issuer used by production.

The chain of failure was:

1. The staging loop was enabled with production issuer settings.
2. It generated repeated renewal attempts.
3. The issuer began rate limiting.
4. Production renewal attempts were delayed until after expiry.
5. The audio host certificate expired at 08:41 UTC.

This was not a Kubernetes availability issue. It was a configuration and change isolation problem. The production and staging certificate request patterns were not sufficiently separated, and we did not have a guardrail that prevented a staging loop from consuming issuer capacity needed by prod.

## What went well

- `KubeClientCertificateExpiration` fired before the expiry, which gave us some warning.
- The edge symptoms were clear and matched the report from users, so we could confirm scope quickly.
- Grafana made the failure mode obvious. The edge success rate dropped at the same time the TLS errors rose.
- Once the staging loop was identified, stopping it was straightforward and immediately reduced pressure on the issuer.
- The new certificate propagated without needing any service redeploys.

## What went badly

- The warning window was too short for the amount of human response needed.
- We did not notice the staging loop before it started affecting production renewal capacity.
- The alert pointed to a certificate nearing expiry, but there was no earlier page for issuer rate limiting pressure.
- We relied on a shared issuer path without enough isolation between staging and production behavior.
- The incident created a confusing initial picture because the service itself was healthy, while the transport layer was failing.
