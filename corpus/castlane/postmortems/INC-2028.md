# INC-2028: One very large show held the feed rebuild lock for 40 minutes
2025-11-25, 09:10 to 10:03 UTC, sev3, IC: zi, author: zi

## Summary

At 09:10 UTC, a creator updated artwork for a show with about 12,000 episodes. That change triggered a full feed rebuild in `feed-builder`, which took the `feedlock:<show_id>` lock and began rebuilding every episode variant from scratch. While that job was running, the same show received additional update events, and our retry loop kept requeueing the rebuild work instead of backing off. Those retries occupied most of the worker pool for `feed-builder`, so other rebuilds started to queue behind the stuck work.

We first saw `FeedBuildLagHigh` at 09:18 UTC. The lag grew steadily until the long rebuild finished and the queued retries drained. The incident ended once the lock was released and the backlog cleared at 10:03 UTC.

This was not a data loss issue. It was a capacity and queueing failure in the rebuild path. The underlying problem was that we treated an artwork change for a very large show like a normal rebuild, even though that path is expensive and serialised by design.

## Impact

- Feed rebuild lag increased from a normal baseline of under 2 minutes to a peak of 41 minutes.
- 1,284 feed rebuild jobs were delayed across 317 shows.
- 46 shows had RSS updates delayed by more than 20 minutes.
- 9 private feed refreshes were also delayed because they share the same rebuild workers.
- `FeedBuildLagHigh` fired once and stayed active for 44 minutes.
- No episodes were lost, and no published audio was affected.

## Timeline (UTC)

- 09:10, artwork update for the 12,000 episode show was received by `feed-builder`.
- 09:11, the full rebuild started and acquired `feedlock:<show_id>`.
- 09:14, additional update events for the same show arrived and entered the retry loop.
- 09:18, `FeedBuildLagHigh` fired in Alertmanager.
- 09:21, queue depth on the rebuild workers began to climb sharply.
- 09:28, we confirmed the same show was holding the lock and generating repeated retries.
- 09:35, we paused nonessential manual reruns and watched the queue drain only slowly.
- 09:44, the full rebuild completed and released `feedlock:<show_id>`.
- 09:45, the retry loop stopped blocking workers and lag started dropping.
- 10:03, feed rebuild lag returned to normal and the alert cleared.

## Root cause

The immediate cause was a full rebuild triggered by an artwork change on a very large show. That rebuild needed to process all episodes and stayed inside the lock for 40 minutes. We use `feedlock:<show_id>` to prevent concurrent rebuilds for the same show, which is correct, but the implementation did not distinguish between a long running rebuild and a small update that should wait quietly.

The deeper issue was the retry loop. When a rebuild for the same show found the lock already held, we retried quickly and repeatedly. That was acceptable when most shows were small, but for a show this large it became a self-inflicted queue storm. Instead of letting the blocked work age out, we kept feeding the worker pool with retries that could not make progress. The result was head-of-line blocking for unrelated shows.

We also had no guardrail for rebuild duration on very large shows. We knew this show was an outlier, but the rebuild path still used the same execution model as ordinary feeds. In practice, that meant one unusual metadata change could monopolise worker capacity.

## What went well

- `FeedBuildLagHigh` alerted quickly enough for us to inspect the queue before customer reports started.
- The worker logs made the lock contention clear, which shortened diagnosis.
- We had a clean recovery once the rebuild finished. There was no bad state to unwind.
- We were able to confirm the issue from live metrics and queue depth without needing any manual database intervention.

## What went badly

- We did not have a separate path for huge shows, so a routine artwork change became a full rebuild.
- Our retry behaviour was too aggressive and turned a single slow job into queue pressure for everyone else.
- The lock was correct, but our use of it was too coarse for this workload.
- We had no alert on rebuild duration per show, so we only noticed the impact after global lag had already grown.
- We relied on worker saturation as a symptom rather than preventing the saturation in the first place.
