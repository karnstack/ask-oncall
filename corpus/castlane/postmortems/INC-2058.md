# INC-2058: Edge capacity halved during a node pool upgrade

2026-01-13, 18:42-19:11 UTC, sev2, IC: @hq, author: @hq

## Summary

During a node pool upgrade in prod-us-east, the cloud provider drained four edge nodes at the same time. `cdn-edge` had no PodDisruptionBudget, so Kubernetes was allowed to evict enough pods to cut serving capacity in half.

**Do not assume the upgrade controller will preserve enough spare capacity for stateless workloads.** We saw `KubeNodeNotReady` and `KubeDeploymentReplicasMismatch` at the same time, but the alerts only told us the cluster was mid-change, not that customer traffic was already degraded.

The incident lasted 9 minutes. Traffic recovered after the upgrade wave finished and pods rescheduled onto remaining nodes.

## Impact

- Edge capacity dropped from 24 ready `cdn-edge` pods to 12 ready pods.
- p99 request latency for audio and feed requests through the edge layer tripled, from 180 ms to 540 ms.
- 6.8 percent of requests to the edge layer returned 5xx or timed out during the worst 3-minute window.
- 1.9 million requests were affected across podcast audio delivery and feed fetches.
- No data was lost, and no creator payouts or feed contents were changed.

**Customer-facing effect was real but contained.** This was a sev2 because the edge tier still served traffic, but with a clear latency and error spike.

## Root cause

The root cause was a bad interaction between a node pool upgrade strategy and an application with no PodDisruptionBudget.

The upgrade drained four nodes at once. That was acceptable for the node pool itself, but not for `cdn-edge`, which had no guardrail to limit voluntary disruption. Because the deployment had many replicas spread across those nodes, the drain event evicted enough pods to cut serving capacity in half.

The missing PDB was the direct control gap. The platform change was the trigger.

**This was preventable.** A simple PDB on every stateless deployment would have forced the upgrade to proceed more gradually or wait for enough replacement capacity before continuing.

## What went well

- Alerting fired quickly on `KubeNodeNotReady` and `KubeDeploymentReplicasMismatch`.
- Grafana made the traffic and latency drop obvious within minutes.
- The IC moved fast from symptom checking to the upgrade path.
- The edge team and platform team stayed in one thread and kept decisions tight.
- Recovery was automatic once the upgrade wave completed.

## What went badly

- No PodDisruptionBudget existed for `cdn-edge`.
- The node pool upgrade drained four nodes at once without a workload-aware constraint.
- We did not have a pre-check that blocked upgrades when a stateless deployment lacked a PDB.
- We relied on replica count alone as a safety signal, which was not enough under simultaneous drains.
- The first oncall response focused on pod restarts before the drain pattern was confirmed.

**Do not treat "stateless" as "safe to evict in bulk".** Stateless services still need disruption limits.
