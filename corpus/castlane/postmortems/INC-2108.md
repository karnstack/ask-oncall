# INC-2108: Transcode backlog after a network moved 900 shows

2026-04-07, 15:10-23:40 UTC, sev3, IC: @lm, author: @vi

## Summary

A podcast network moved 900 shows, about 41,000 episodes, into Castlane in one afternoon. The upload spike hit `transcoder` harder than expected. `TranscodeQueueBacklog` fired, then `KubeHpaMaxedOut` on the transcoder deployment. The queue kept growing because the HPA had already reached 30 replicas and could not add more workers fast enough. New uploads from other creators sat behind the migration work and some waited up to 5 hours before transcode started.

We mitigated by adding priority lanes in the transcode queue so normal creator uploads could bypass the bulk migration backlog. That reduced wait times while the migration drained. No data loss. No failed transcodes after retry. Episode publishing resumed normally once the backlog cleared.

## Impact

- 900 shows migrated in one afternoon.
- About 41,000 episodes entered the transcode queue.
- Queue peak: 39,000 pending jobs.
- HPA ceiling reached: 30 transcoder pods.
- New uploads from other creators waited up to 5 hours for transcode start.
- 3,200 uploads were delayed past the normal same-day window.
- Feed rebuilds lagged behind transcode completion for several hours, but feeds recovered after the queue drained.

## Root cause

The migration used the same transcode path as normal creator uploads. We treated 41,000 episodes as ordinary ingress and let them enter the same queue with no admission control or priority separation.

The transcoder HPA reacted correctly to load, but the ceiling was too low for the burst shape. At 30 pods, worker capacity was still below incoming job rate. This was not a code crash, a Redis failure, or a storage problem. It was a capacity and queueing problem caused by an unusually large one-day migration.

The missing control was prioritization. Bulk migration jobs and creator uploads were equal in the queue. That let the migration crowd out ordinary work for hours.

## What went well

- `TranscodeQueueBacklog` alerted early.
- `KubeHpaMaxedOut` gave a clear signal that scaling had stopped helping.
- Oncall quickly identified the migration as the dominant source of load.
- Priority lanes were simple to implement and safe to roll out.
- No transcode data was lost.
- No manual pod restarts were needed.

## What went badly

- We had no migration throttle for a burst of this size.
- We allowed bulk imported episodes to compete with normal uploads.
- The HPA ceiling was reached before the backlog peaked.
- We did not have a tested runbook for prioritizing creator uploads during a large migration.
- Creator-facing delays were long enough to be visible and annoying.
