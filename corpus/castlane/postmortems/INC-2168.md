# INC-2168: July payout batch delayed by a provider outage
2026-07-15, 06:10-10:01 UTC, sev2, IC: nd, author: nd

## Summary

The July payout batch was delayed because the payment provider had an outage during the scheduled run. The batch started at 06:00 UTC as expected, then began failing on provider calls at 06:10 UTC. The job stopped itself when failures reached 5 percent, which is the current safety limit in `payouts-worker`.

`PayoutBatchLate` fired at 08:00 UTC. We paused further retry work while we waited for the provider to recover. The provider outage ended at 09:05 UTC. We reran the batch at 09:20 UTC, and it completed at 10:01 UTC.

No payout data was lost. No duplicate payouts were sent. The issue was limited to delayed processing for the July batch.

## Impact

- 18,442 creator payout rows were due in this batch.
- 17,503 rows completed in the original run before the worker stopped.
- 939 rows were left for the rerun after the provider recovered.
- The batch finished 4 hours and 1 minute after the scheduled start, instead of within the normal 20 to 30 minute window.
- Creator-facing payout visibility was delayed for the affected batch until 10:01 UTC.
- Support saw 11 internal follow-ups from creators asking about payout status between 08:15 and 10:30 UTC.

## Root cause

The root cause was an outage at the payment provider during the scheduled payout window. `payouts-worker` depends on that provider for transfer creation and status checks. When the provider began returning failures, the batch processed some rows successfully, then hit the 5 percent failure threshold and stopped itself.

That stop condition worked as designed. It prevented a long stream of partial failures and reduced the risk of repeated bad requests. The delay happened because the provider outage lasted long enough that the first scheduled run could not finish.

A secondary factor was the batch schedule itself. The run starts at 06:00 UTC on the 15th, which left no slack for a provider outage of this length. Once the batch stopped, it had to wait for manual rerun after the provider recovered.

## What went well

- The batch failed safely. It stopped at the configured threshold instead of continuing to hammer the provider.
- `PayoutBatchLate` fired on time and gave us a clear signal that the batch had not completed.
- The worker exited cleanly, so we were able to inspect state without repairing a half-running job.
- The rerun completed without manual data fixes.
- Communication in `#oncall-payouts` stayed short and accurate. We kept support informed while waiting for recovery.

## What went badly

- The batch had no automatic recovery path once the provider outage ended.
- We did not have an earlier alert for provider-side failures. The first Castlane alert was `PayoutBatchLate` at 08:00 UTC.
- The batch window had no built-in slack for a provider outage of this duration.
- We needed a manual decision to rerun, which added 15 minutes after provider recovery.
- The failure threshold is useful, but it also means a short provider outage can turn into a full batch delay.
