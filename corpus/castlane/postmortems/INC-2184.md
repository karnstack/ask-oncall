# INC-2184: Agency laptop lost

2026-08-11, 14:20 UTC to 18:05 UTC, sev2, IC: sec-ir, author: sec-lead

## Summary

On 2026-08-11, an agency engineer reported that a company-managed laptop was lost while travelling. The device belonged to a contractor on the platform rotation. The laptop was configured with full-disk encryption and was not enrolled in any privileged production access workflow beyond normal contractor access.

We treated this as a security incident because the device had active sessions and cached credentials at the time it was reported missing. The immediate response was to revoke access, rotate exposed credentials, and verify whether any sensitive systems were accessed from unusual locations. We found no evidence of misuse or unauthorized access.

This incident is restricted distribution because it concerns security controls, contractor access, and internal response timings.

## Impact

- 1 laptop lost in transit.
- 1 contractor account had active sessions revoked.
- 6 access tokens and SSH sessions invalidated within 40 minutes of report receipt.
- 0 confirmed unauthorized logins.
- 0 confirmed access to production systems from the missing device.
- 0 customer impact, no service degradation, no data loss.

The incident did create a short window of elevated risk for internal access paths used by the agency engineer. That risk was contained before any suspicious activity appeared in audit logs.

## Root cause

The direct cause was physical loss of a laptop while travelling. The device itself was protected with full-disk encryption, which reduced the likelihood of offline data exposure. The security issue was not the loss event alone, but the presence of active sessions and cached credentials on a portable device.

The contributing control gap was that contractor device posture was not being checked consistently before and during access to internal systems. We had account-level controls, but we did not have a strong enough periodic posture verification for contractor endpoints. That made the response dependent on manual detection after the report instead of earlier enforcement.

## What went well

- The contractor reported the loss quickly.
- The security and platform responders coordinated within minutes.
- Session revocation and credential invalidation completed within 40 minutes of the report.
- Audit logs were available and sufficient to check for misuse.
- Full-disk encryption was enabled on the device, which reduced exposure risk.
- No production service needed a config change or emergency rollback.

## What went badly

- We did not have a strong automated posture check for contractor devices before this event.
- The account had more active sessions than we want for a portable endpoint.
- The reporting path depended on the contractor noticing the loss and contacting the right channel.
- Our review process for contractor endpoint health was not enforced tightly enough for the platform rotation.
