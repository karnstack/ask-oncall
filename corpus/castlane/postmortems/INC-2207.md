# INC-2207: private-feed tokens written to application logs

RESTRICTED. Security team only. Do not paste into channels outside
`#inc-2207`. Ask sec-lead before sharing any part of it.

Date: 2026-09-10 14:20 to 2026-09-15 12:45 UTC. Severity: sev2.
IC: sec-lead. Detection: sec-ir. Author: sec-lead.

## Summary

Private-feed tokens for 1,912 subscribers were written to the private-feeds
application log for five days. A logging library upgrade in private-feeds
v2.31.0 (deployed 2026-09-10 14:20) made the request logger print the full
request URL, including the `?token=pft_...` query string, at INFO level. Our
detection rule `secrets-in-logs/private-feed-token` caught it on 2026-09-15
at 04:38.

We stopped the logging, rotated all 1,912 tokens without a grace period, and
purged the affected log indices and their snapshots. We found no sign that
anyone outside the company read the logs.

As a precaution we paused the September payout batch for six hours, so
creator payouts went out late on Tuesday 2026-09-15. payouts-worker had the
same library upgrade, and the batch handles bank account references; we held
it until we had confirmed its logs were clean.

## Impact

- 1,912 private-feed subscribers had to update their feed link. They received
  the new-link email between 06:40 and 10:15 UTC. Until they did, their apps
  got 401s.
- Tokens were readable by anyone with access to the log store (engineering,
  three contractor accounts with log read access, the log vendor's support
  under contract). Access logs show 41 searches touching the indices, all by
  employees debugging unrelated issues.
- September creator payouts (48,210 payouts) started at 12:05 UTC instead
  of 06:00 UTC.

## Root cause

The new request logger logs `req.url` instead of `req.path`. Nobody reviewing
the dependency PR (PLAT-4388) knew the default had changed. private-feeds is
the only service that puts a secret in a URL, by design: podcast apps cannot
send headers, so the token has to be in the feed link (ADR-0033).

## What went well

- Detection worked: the rule fired within hours of the first daily scan of
  the new index pattern.
- Payouts held the batch in three minutes and told `#incidents` without
  saying why.

## What went badly

- Five days before detection. The rule only scanned indices older than 24
  hours and ran daily.
- Three contractor accounts had log read access they did not need.
