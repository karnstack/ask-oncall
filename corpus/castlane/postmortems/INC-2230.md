# INC-2230: Alert noise after every page opened an incident

2026-10-06, 2026-10-01 00:00 UTC to 2026-10-05 18:40 UTC, sev4, IC: @rs, author: @rs

## Summary

After the 2026-10-01 policy change that every page opens an incident, a flapping `KubeJobFailed` alert on `stats-hourly` created a disproportionate amount of noise. The job failed intermittently, Alertmanager fired on each failure, and the new policy turned each page into a separate incident. Over five days this opened 23 incidents, most of which were duplicates for the same underlying job failure pattern.

The alert was technically valid, but the page policy made the impact much worse. The immediate fix was to adjust the job retry behavior so transient failures were retried in place, and to raise the alert `for` duration so a single missed run would not page. The long term fix is to treat batch job flapping differently from stateful service failures.

## Impact

- 23 incidents were opened in 5 days.
- 19 of those incidents were duplicates for the same alert pattern.
- 7 on-call handoffs spent time re-triaging the same job failure.
- Median time spent per duplicate page was 9 minutes, or about 3.5 engineer-hours total.
- `stats-hourly` missed 4 scheduled runs before retry tuning, which delayed fresh download metrics in the feed stats pipeline by up to 18 minutes.
- No customer-facing outage was confirmed, and no data was lost.

## Root cause

The root cause was the combination of a flapping batch job and the new incident policy. `stats-hourly` was failing on the first attempt due to transient startup timing in its pod, then usually succeeding on a later attempt. Before 2026-10-01 this created an alert, but not an incident storm. After the policy change, each page opened a separate incident automatically, so one noisy alert became 23 incidents over five days.

The alert rule was too sensitive for a job with known transient startup failure modes. The `for` duration was short enough that a brief failure was enough to page before the job’s natural recovery pattern could resolve it.

The relevant check was effectively equivalent to:

```promql
kube_job_status_failed{job="stats-hourly", namespace="feeds"} > 0
```

That was too eager for this workload. We needed to tolerate short-lived failure states and only page on sustained failure.

## What went well

- We had enough telemetry in Grafana to confirm the failure pattern quickly.
- The job’s failure mode was consistent, which made the root cause easy to isolate.
- The change to retry behavior was small and safe to roll out.
- Raising the alert `for` duration reduced noise without hiding a real sustained failure.
- Coordination between platform and download-stats was quick, and the same dashboard was used by everyone during triage.

Checklist that helped during triage:
- Confirm whether the job eventually succeeded.
- Check if the failure is repeated across several runs or isolated to one node.
- Compare the alert duration with the job’s expected retry window.
- Verify whether the page policy is amplifying a known transient condition.

## What went badly

- The policy change made an already noisy alert operationally expensive.
- We did not have a documented threshold for when batch job flaps should page versus notify.
- The incident stream made it harder to distinguish new failures from duplicates.
- We did not catch the retry weakness before the policy rollout.
- The alert rule was treated as correct because it was syntactically valid, even though it was too sensitive for this workload.
