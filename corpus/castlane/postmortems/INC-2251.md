# INC-2251: Private feeds a few minutes behind after replica lag

2026-10-13, 09:14 UTC to 10:02 UTC, sev3, IC: tw, author: ne

## Summary

At 09:14 UTC we noticed that private feed subscribers were seeing new episodes later than expected. The delay was not a full outage, but updates that should have appeared within about a minute were showing up several minutes late.

The issue was limited to private feeds backed by the `feeds_ro` read path in `private-feeds`. The root cause was an analytics query running on a `pg-feeds` replica with `hot_standby_feedback` enabled. That query was long enough to hold back replay on the replica, which allowed replication lag to build until it reached about 4 minutes. Since private feed reads depend on the replica for fresh show data, the feed responses were stale until replay caught up.

We mitigated the incident by moving the analytics query to a dedicated replica that is not on the serving path. After the move, replica lag dropped back under 10 seconds and private feed freshness recovered.

A small definition here, since I used the term above: replica lag is the delay between changes being written on the primary database and those changes becoming visible on a read replica. For private feeds, that delay directly affects how quickly a new episode appears in a subscriber's feed.

## Impact

- Private feed updates were delayed for 2,840 subscribers across 19 shows.
- The maximum observed lag on the affected `pg-feeds` replica was 4 minutes and 12 seconds.
- The median delay for new episodes appearing in private feeds during the incident window was about 3 minutes.
- 7 support messages were posted in `#ask-oncall` and `#feeds-dev` asking why recent episodes were not visible yet.
- No data was lost, and no public feeds were affected.

## Root cause

The root cause was a long-running analytics query on a `pg-feeds` replica that was also serving `private-feeds` reads.

That replica had `hot_standby_feedback` enabled so read queries would not be canceled when vacuum needed to clean up old row versions. In practice, that setting can be helpful for avoiding query cancellations, but it also means a long-running query can keep the replica from advancing replay normally. In our case, the query was doing broad aggregation over recent feed activity and stayed open long enough to block replay progress.

Because `private-feeds` reads from `feeds_ro`, any meaningful replica lag shows up as stale feed content. The service was not broken, but it was serving old state until the replica caught up.

The immediate fix was to remove the analytics workload from the serving replica by moving it to a dedicated replica. After that change, the serving replica stopped accumulating lag and private feed freshness returned to normal.

## What went well

- `ask-oncall` surfaced a user-visible symptom quickly, before support volume built up.
- Grafana made the lag pattern obvious once we looked at `pg-feeds` replication metrics.
- The `private-feeds` service behaved correctly given stale replica data, which made the issue easier to isolate to the database layer.
- We had a spare replica available for the analytics workload, so the mitigation was straightforward and low risk.
- Communication in `#feeds-dev` was clear, and people kept the incident updates focused on the actual symptom and fix.

## What went badly

- We had allowed a reporting query to run on the same replica that serves production reads for private feeds.
- We did not have a guardrail that prevented a non-serving workload from being pointed at the serving replica.
- The behavior of `hot_standby_feedback` was not obvious to the people who own the reporting job, so the query looked harmless until lag accumulated.
- We did not have an alert specifically for replica lag on the serving path, so the incident began as a user report rather than a page.
