# INC-2291: feed errors from PgBouncer pool saturation during a viral episode

Date: 2026-10-26, 03:04 to 04:20 UTC. Severity: sev2. IC: tw. Author: mbe.
Status: action items in progress.

## Summary

One episode of a top-ranked show was shared widely late on Sunday in the
Americas. Feed fetches rose to about nine times normal in prod-us-east and
then in prod-eu-west. feed-builder scaled to its HPA maximum of 40 pods and
filled the pgbouncer-feeds pools. With three PgBouncer pods at
`default_pool_size = 40`, PgBouncer tried to open 480 server connections
against a Postgres `max_connections` of 400. Postgres refused the rest with
SQLSTATE 53300, and feed requests failed instead of queueing.

The runbook told the on-call engineer to scale PgBouncer out, which made it
worse. The fix was to lower the pool to 25 on every pod at runtime. It was
posted in `#inc-2291` at 03:31 and applied in prod-eu-west at 03:52.

## Impact

- prod-us-east: 4 to 11 percent of `/feed` requests failed between 03:04 and
  03:36 (32 minutes). Podcast apps retry, so most listeners saw a delay, not
  an error. About 210,000 failed feed requests.
- prod-eu-west: elevated errors from 03:40 to 03:52.
- No data loss. No private feeds affected beyond the same error rate.
- feed-builder lag peaked at 9 minutes; new episodes published in that window
  appeared in feeds up to 12 minutes late.

## Root cause

PgBouncer opens a separate pool for each database and user pair on each pod.
The safe pool size depends on the pod count:

```
pods x default_pool_size x users <= max_connections - headroom
2 x 40 x 4 = 320   (when runbook v3 was written, 2025-08-14)
3 x 40 x 4 = 480   (since PLAT-4410 added a third pod, 2026-09-08)
```

PLAT-4410 added the third pod for resilience and did not change the pool
size. The pools never filled until this traffic spike, so nothing failed for
seven weeks.

## Contributing factors

- Runbook v3's mitigation ("confirm the pool is 40, then scale out") was
  wrong once the pod count changed. Scaling out adds connections, not
  database capacity.
- `#ask-oncall` is built from a nightly index (02:00 UTC). At 03:43 the
  workaround from 03:31 was not in it, and v3 was the only PgBouncer runbook
  it knew. The answer was a faithful summary of a wrong page.
- Nothing alerts on Postgres connection count. We saw it only because rs
  looked.

## What went well

- rs went to the Postgres side early and found the real limit.
- The workaround was written as copy-paste commands with the arithmetic next
  to them. ok applied it in prod-eu-west in three minutes.
- ok read the incident channel before acting on the `#ask-oncall` answer.

## What went badly

- 9 minutes between the first 53300 and someone looking at Postgres.
- Scaling out cost about 13 minutes of extra errors.
- The second region was paged separately and nearly repeated the scale-out.
